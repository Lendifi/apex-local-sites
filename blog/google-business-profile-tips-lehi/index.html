Google Business Profile tips for Lehi businesses: 9 fixes you can do this week

By Sam Johnsen, Apex Local Sites6 min readUtah County, UtahPublished October 30, 2026
The short versionGoogle ranks local results on relevance, distance, and prominence. Verify your profile, use your real name with no keywords, pick the right category, set an honest service area, fill in every field, add real photos, get and reply to reviews, and post regularly.

When someone in Lehi searches "plumber near me" or "auto repair Lehi," the first thing they usually see isn't a website. It's the map and the list of Google Business Profiles under it. For a lot of local businesses, that profile is doing more selling than the website is.

The good news: most of what helps a profile is free, and you can do it yourself. Here are nine fixes for Lehi and Utah County businesses, based on Google's own published guidelines, in plain English.

How Google picks who shows up

Google says local results are mainly based on three things (source: Google Business Profile Help):

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches what someone searched.
  • Distance: how far you are from the person searching.
  • Prominence: how well known you are, which includes things like reviews and links to your business from other websites.

Google also says plainly that there is no way to pay for a better local ranking. You can't change distance. But relevance and prominence are mostly in your hands, and that's what the fixes below are about.

1. Claim and verify your profile

If you haven't verified your profile, start there. Google says verification tells it you're authorized to represent the business, which makes it more likely to show up. Search your business on Google, and if you see "Own this business?", follow the steps.

2. Use your real business name, with no extra keywords

It's tempting to name your profile "Best Lehi Plumbing | 24/7 Emergency Plumber." Don't. Google's guidelines for representing your business say your name should match your real-world name as used on your signs, truck, and website, and that adding things like taglines, service descriptions, or locations isn't permitted and could get your profile suspended. A suspended profile disappears from the map entirely.

3. Pick the right primary category

Your category tells Google what you are. Google's tip: choose categories that finish the sentence "this business is a..." rather than "this business has a..." Use the most specific one that fits ("Landscaper," "Roofing contractor," "Auto repair shop," "Chiropractor") and as few as you need. Don't add categories just as keywords.

4. Set your service area the right way

If you drive to your customers (contractors, mobile mechanics, cleaners), Google says you should set a service area and hide your address, especially if you work from home. List the places you really serve, for example Lehi, Saratoga Springs, Eagle Mountain, American Fork, Highland, and Alpine. Keep it honest. Google's guidelines say the overall service area generally shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving from where you're based.

If customers come to you (a shop, clinic, or studio), show your full street address. Google doesn't allow P.O. boxes or virtual offices as a business address.

5. Fill in every field you can

Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local results. That means:

  • Regular hours, plus special hours for holidays (think Thanksgiving, Christmas, Pioneer Day)
  • Your services, each with a short plain-language description
  • A business description that explains what you do and who you serve (no links and no sales promos in this field; Google doesn't allow them there)
  • A local phone number you control, and the right website link
  • Attributes that apply to you, like accessibility or payment options

6. Add real photos, and keep adding them

Show your actual work: finished jobs, your crew, your truck with your logo, your shop or office. Google encourages photos and videos to show customers what you offer. Real photos build trust far faster than stock images. A good habit is snapping two or three photos at the end of every job and uploading the best ones every week or two.

7. Ask every happy customer for a review

Reviews are part of prominence, and Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. The easiest way to get them:

  1. Get your review link from your profile (look for an option like "Ask for reviews" or "Get more reviews").
  2. Text or email it right after a job goes well, while they're still happy.
  3. Keep it simple: "Thanks again for having us out. If you have 30 seconds, a Google review really helps a small local business like ours."

Ask every customer, not only the ones you expect to be glowing. Never offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, and never post reviews for yourself.

8. Reply to every review, good or bad

Google says replying shows customers you value their feedback. For good reviews, a short thank-you that mentions the job is plenty. For bad ones, stay calm, don't argue, and offer to make it right offline: "Sorry to hear this. Please call me at [number] so I can fix it." Future customers read your replies as much as the reviews.

9. Post updates regularly

Profile posts are short updates that show up on your listing. They show you're active. Ideas that work well in Utah County:

  • Seasonal: sprinkler blowouts in the fall, furnace checks before the first cold snap, A/C tune-ups in spring, snow removal sign-ups
  • Recent work: a before-and-after photo with one sentence about the job and the city
  • Tips: one useful tip customers would actually want
  • Offers and events: keep any promotion clear and honest about the terms

Bonus: make your website and profile agree

Your profile and your website should back each other up. Same business name, same phone number, same services, same cities. Make sure the website link on your profile goes to a page that loads fast on a phone and shows your number at the top. If your site is broken or slow, you're sending your best leads to a dead end. Our 10-minute website check shows you how to spot that.

A simple weekly routine

  • Monday: post one update (a recent job or a seasonal tip)
  • After each job: send the review link
  • Friday: reply to any new reviews and upload a few new photos
  • Once a month: check hours, services, and your website link
