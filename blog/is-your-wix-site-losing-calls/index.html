Website check-up

Is your Wix site losing calls? A 10-minute check

By Sam Johnsen, Apex Local Sites6 min readUtah County, UtahPublished October 30, 2026
The short versionThe platform is rarely the problem. Neglect is. Check your site on your phone for: tap-to-call at the top, no "Not secure" warning, a site that actually loads from your Google profile, a decent mobile speed score, and a form that really reaches you.

First, let's be fair to Wix. It's a capable website builder, and plenty of local businesses run great sites on it. The same goes for Squarespace, GoDaddy, and WordPress. The platform is rarely the real problem.

The problem is usually that the site was set up once, a few years ago, by someone in a hurry, and nobody has checked on it since. Phone layouts changed, a form email went stale, a subscription lapsed, and the business owner had no idea, because a broken website doesn't call you to complain. It just quietly sends customers to the next company on Google.

When we review local business sites around Utah County, the same handful of issues show up again and again, on every platform. Here is a 10-minute check you can do yourself, right now, with just your phone.

The 10-minute website check

1. Open your site on your phone, not your computer

Most owners only ever look at their site on a desktop. Your customers are mostly on phones. Pull up your site the way they would: search your business name on Google on your phone and tap through.

  • Can you see your phone number without scrolling?
  • Can you tap it to call?
  • Is any text too small to read, or are buttons too close together to tap?

Wix and other builders often have a separate mobile layout. If someone edited the desktop version and never looked at the mobile one, things can end up overlapping, hidden, or out of order.

2. Look for the "Not secure" warning

Check the address bar. You want a padlock or "https" with no warnings. If the browser says "Not secure," or shows a full-page security warning, a lot of people will hit the back button immediately. This often happens when a security certificate expired or a custom domain was connected incorrectly.

3. Make sure the site actually loads

It sounds obvious, but this is one of the most common problems we find. Search your business on Google, tap the website link on your Google profile, and see what happens. Watch out for:

  • An error page, or a page saying the site isn't connected
  • A "domain for sale" or parked page
  • A builder's default placeholder page instead of your site

If your builder subscription or your domain renewal lapsed, the site or the address can stop working, and no one tells you. Check that the card on file is current for both.

4. Test your speed

Go to Google's free PageSpeed Insights, paste in your website address, and look at the mobile score. You don't need to understand every line. Red scores mean the site is slow on phones, and slow sites lose impatient visitors. The usual culprits are huge photos, video backgrounds, and piles of add-on apps.

5. Send yourself a test form

Fill out your own contact or quote form with your name and "TEST" in the message. Then check:

  • Did it arrive at all?
  • Did it go to an inbox you actually read, or an old email from three employees ago?
  • Did it land in spam?

A form that silently goes nowhere is the most expensive problem on this list, because the customer thinks they reached you.

6. Look for leftover template text

Scroll every page for things like "Lorem ipsum," "Your Business Name," "123 Main Street," a fake phone number like 555-0100, or stock "About Us" text that isn't about you. Customers notice, and it makes a real business look abandoned.

7. Check the details that go stale

  • Is the copyright year in the footer several years old?
  • Are your hours, service area, and phone number still correct?
  • Do you list services you no longer offer, or leave off ones you do?
  • Does the site still say "Coming soon" anywhere?

8. Say what you do and where, in plain words

Google matches searches to the words on your page. If someone in Lehi searches "sprinkler repair Lehi" and your site only says "Quality outdoor solutions," you're making it hard for Google and for the customer. List your actual services and the actual cities you serve.

9. Check your web address

Is your site on your own domain (yourcompany.com), or on a free builder address with the builder's name in it? A free subdomain works, but it looks less established and is harder for customers to remember. If you do have your own domain, make sure it's registered in your name.

10. Know who has the keys

Do you know the login for your website and for your domain? If the person who built it moved on, get access back now, before something breaks. This is the number one thing that turns a 10-minute fix into a 2-week headache.

What to do with what you find

Write down every "no" from the list above. Then sort them:

  • Fix today: site not loading, "Not secure" warning, broken form, wrong phone number. These are costing you calls right now.
  • Fix this month: slow mobile speed, leftover template text, out-of-date hours and services.
  • Plan for later: moving to your own domain, adding city pages, a refresh of the design.

Plenty of these you can fix yourself inside your Wix (or other builder) dashboard in an afternoon. If you'd rather not, or if the list is long, that's where a pro earns their fee.

Do you need to switch platforms?

Usually not as a first step. If your site is on Wix and the problems are setup problems, fixing the setup is often enough. A rebuild makes sense when the site is slow no matter what you trim, the design is hard to update, nobody has the login, or you want features like missed-call text-back and instant lead alerts tied in.
