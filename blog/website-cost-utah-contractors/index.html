Website costs

How much does a website cost for a Utah contractor?

By Sam Johnsen, Apex Local Sites6 min readUtah County, UtahPublished October 30, 2026
The short versionDIY sites run about $0 to $450 to build plus monthly fees. Agencies typically charge about $1,200 for a one-page site and $3,850 for five pages (median, per Lindo's 2026 report). What matters most: you own the domain, edits are covered, and the site turns visitors into calls.

If you run a landscaping crew in Lehi, a roofing company in American Fork, or a fencing business in Saratoga Springs, you have probably been quoted wildly different prices for a website. One person says $300. Another says $8,000. A big agency wants a monthly retainer that costs more than your truck payment.

Here is a plain-language breakdown of what a contractor website actually costs in 2026, what you are paying for, and how to tell a fair quote from a bad one. I build sites for Utah County businesses, so I'll also tell you exactly what we charge. No "contact us for pricing" games.

What you're actually paying for

Every website price is some mix of the same pieces. When two quotes look far apart, it's usually because one includes more of these than the other.

  • The build. Design, writing, photos, and setting up the pages. This is the one-time part.
  • Your domain. The yourcompany.com address. Forbes Advisor puts domain registration at roughly $10 to $35 a year.
  • Hosting and security. Keeping the site online and showing the padlock (SSL) so browsers don't warn people your site is "not secure."
  • Changes after launch. New photos, a price change, holiday hours. Somebody has to do it, and that somebody is either you or someone you pay.
  • Lead capture. Tap-to-call buttons, a quote form that actually reaches you, and alerts so you can respond fast.

The three ways contractors usually get a site

1. Build it yourself

Website builders like Wix, Squarespace, and GoDaddy let you drag and drop a site together. Forbes Advisor estimates a DIY informational site at about $0 to $450 to build, plus $15 to $150 a month for hosting and apps.

The real cost is your time. If you bill $75 or more an hour on a job site, spending three Saturdays fighting a template is not free. DIY works well if you enjoy it and will keep it updated. It works badly if the site gets built once and forgotten.

2. Hire a freelancer or agency for a one-time project

This is the traditional route. You pay a lump sum, get a site, and then you're on your own for changes (or you pay hourly for them).

3. Done-for-you with a monthly plan

This is a smaller upfront fee plus a monthly fee that covers hosting, security, and ongoing edits. You don't touch the site; you text your builder when something changes. For a busy contractor, this tends to be the option where the site actually stays current.

Hidden costs nobody puts in the quote

  • Who owns the domain. If your web person registered yourcompany.com in their own name, you may not be able to leave without losing your address. Always ask that it be registered in your name.
  • Renewals. Domains and builder subscriptions renew every year. If a card expires and nobody notices, the site or the address can quietly stop working. That's a lost-calls problem, not just a tech problem.
  • "Small" changes. Many one-time projects charge hourly for every edit after launch. A few tweaks a year can add up.
  • Missed leads. A cheap site that doesn't show your phone number at the top on a phone, or a form that sends to an old email address, costs you jobs you never hear about.

What a Utah contractor website really needs

You don't need 30 pages or fancy animations. You do need a site that gets someone from "I found you on Google" to "I called you" as fast as possible.

  • Your phone number and a tap-to-call button at the top of every page on a phone
  • A clear list of services in plain words ("sprinkler repair," not "outdoor water solutions")
  • The cities you serve, written out: Lehi, Saratoga Springs, Eagle Mountain, American Fork, and so on
  • Real photos of your real work (not stock photos of somebody else's deck)
  • Your Google reviews or a link to them
  • A short quote form that actually reaches you, tested after launch
  • Fast loading on a phone, with the padlock (SSL) showing
  • A link to your site from your Google Business Profile

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

  1. Will the domain be registered in my name?
  2. What exactly is included each month, and what costs extra?
  3. How fast do you make changes when I ask?
  4. Can I see two or three sites you've built for other local businesses?
  5. Is there a contract minimum, and what happens to my site if I leave?
  6. Who do I call when something breaks?

Any honest builder will answer all six without squirming.

The bottom line

For a typical Utah contractor, a solid website costs somewhere between a few hundred dollars (if you build it yourself and value your time at zero) and several thousand (for a custom agency build). The number that matters more is whether the site brings in calls and stays current. A $1,000 site that gets you two extra jobs a month is cheap. A $300 site that loses them is expensive.
