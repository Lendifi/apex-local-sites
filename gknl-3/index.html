Three things to know before your exam

  1. Social Security pays for the exam. You do not.
  2. The doctor does the exam. The state agency decides your claim.
  3. Can't make it? Call the state agency number on your letter right away.

Source: Social Security publication 05-10087, "A Special Examination Is Needed for Your Disability Claim."

What to bring

  • Your appointment letter
  • Photo ID
  • Every medicine you takeIn the original containers
  • Glasses, contact lenses or hearing aids
  • Your cane, walker or other aid

Arrive 15 minutes early. Bring children only if the exam is for the child. From the standard Social Security appointment notice (POMS NL 00705.745). Your letter is the final word.

Common questions

Do I pay for the exam?

No. Social Security pays for the exam it asks for, and may pay some travel costs if you qualify. Your letter explains travel payment.

Will the doctor treat me or prescribe medicine?

No. The doctor only does the exam and gets the information the state agency asked for. They do not prescribe treatment or medication.

Who decides my claim?

The Disability Determination Services office in your state. It reviews the exam report along with everything else in your file, and Social Security sends you a letter with the decision.

Can my own doctor get a copy?

Yes. Fill out the authorization form that came with your appointment letter and send it to the state agency.

What if I need an interpreter?

Social Security provides one for free. Tell the state agency before your appointment, or tell them if you are bringing your own.

What if I disagree with the decision?

You can appeal. The decision letter tells you how.

Answers based on Social Security publication 05-10087 and the standard appointment notice. For anything about your own claim, call the number on your letter or Social Security at 1-800-772-1213 (TTY 1-800-325-0778).
